How it works

Drop it in. Dock it. Done.

  1. 1

    Drop in an .ipa

    Drag a package onto Harbor, or open it from Finder. Harbor checks it first: architecture, encryption, the frameworks it needs and anything a Mac can't do.

  2. 2

    Harbor rebuilds it for the Mac

    Every binary is retargeted to Mac Catalyst, a small runtime is added, and the app gets a Mac icon, a sandbox and a signature. Nothing is emulated.

  3. 3

    Open it like any Mac app

    It's in Applications, Launchpad and Spotlight, with its own Dock icon, window and save data. Remove it from Harbor whenever you like.

Features

Everything an iPhone app expects,
on a Mac that isn't one.

iOS apps assume a touchscreen, a phone-shaped screen, motion sensors and a back camera. Harbor's runtime fills each of those gaps from inside the app, so it behaves as if it's on the device it was made for.

Keys and controllers become touches

Press ⌘⇧K over any app to open the keymap editor. Click to place a button or a joystick, then press a key or a controller button to bind it. Harbor sends real multi-touch events, so games built only for touch just work.

The right window

Windows take the shape of the chosen iPhone or iPad. They can resize with the aspect ratio locked, or freely for iPad apps that support Split View. Landscape-only games open in landscape.

A convincing device

Apps see the iPhone or iPad profile you pick (model, screen and idiom), while macOS and UIKit keep seeing the real Mac.

Motion, without a gyroscope

Tilt-to-steer games get an accelerometer and gyroscope, driven by the arrow keys, a controller's stick or the pointer.

Your Mac's camera

Apps that ask for the iPhone's back camera get your Mac's camera, whether it's built in, Continuity Camera or USB.

Web apps get camera and mic

On the Mac, iOS web views lack getUserMedia and MediaRecorder. Harbor adds them back, with recording to MP4.

A keychain that works

Apps that store logins in the keychain normally fail without an Apple signature. Harbor keeps those items in the app's own sandbox instead.

Know before you convert

Harbor checks each package first: its architecture, whether it's encrypted, the frameworks it needs and anything a Mac can't provide.

A real Mac citizen

Every app has its own sandbox, its own privacy prompts with the app's own wording, and its own Launchpad and Spotlight entry. There's no wrapper app in between.

For the terminal

A CLI that does it all, too.

Harbor.app includes the harbor command-line tool. Use it to check an app's compatibility, convert apps in a script, adjust per-app settings or remove apps along with their data.

It's inside the app at Harbor.app/Contents/Resources/bin/harbor.

zsh
$ harbor inspect TideRunner.ipa

$ harbor settings "Tide Runner" --motion controller

Install

Ready in a minute.

  1. Download Harbor.dmg and open it.
  2. Drag Harbor to Applications.
  3. Open Harbor. The first time, macOS says it can't verify the developer, because Harbor is signed by its builders rather than by Apple. Go to System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. You only need to do this once.
  4. Drop in an .ipa and follow the compatibility report.

Requirements

  • A Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later)
  • macOS 14 Sonoma or later
  • A decrypted .ipa
  • No Xcode and no developer account

Prefer the terminal? Clear the download quarantine with
xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Harbor.app

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Can Harbor run apps I downloaded from the App Store?

No. App Store downloads are encrypted with Apple's FairPlay DRM, and Harbor detects that and refuses them. It converts decrypted packages: your own builds from Xcode, ad hoc and enterprise builds, and open-source apps. Only convert apps you have the right to use.

Is this emulation?

No. Apple silicon Macs run the same Arm code as iPhones and iPads, and macOS ships the iOS frameworks for Mac Catalyst. Harbor rewrites each binary's platform tag so macOS loads it as a Catalyst app, so it runs at full native speed.

Which apps work best?

Games and apps that are self-contained. Features that depend on an Apple-issued signature can't work with a local one: push notifications, iCloud, Game Center, Sign in with Apple and in-app purchases. harbor inspect tells you what to expect before you convert.

How do I play a touch-only game with a keyboard?

Open the game and press ⌘⇧K. Click where a button should go, then press the key or controller button for it. You can also add a joystick for movement. Press Esc to save, and ⌘⇧M turns the mapping off while you type.

Does it change my Mac?

Converted apps go in /Applications, and each one's data stays in its own sandbox container. Harbor doesn't install system extensions or need administrator access. To remove an app, use Harbor or harbor remove "App" --delete-data.

Why does macOS warn me the first time I open it?

Harbor releases are signed ad hoc, not notarized by Apple. Allow it once in System Settings › Privacy & Security. The source code is public, and you can build it yourself with scripts/build.sh.

Is it free?

Yes. It's free and open source under the GNU GPL v3, with no account, no telemetry and no ads.
